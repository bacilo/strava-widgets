# Phase 33: Coverage Integration — Pace Clipping & Segment Signal - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-09-21
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Phase 32's finished detector (`src/analytics/unreliable-segments.ts`) is wired into
`classifyGaps` as a fourth `GapKind`, so the pace chart band, the pace histogram and the per-km
splits clip unreliable segments exactly as they clip recording gaps and pauses today. The
coverage identity is re-proved exact across the new four-way split archive-wide, the always-on
coverage caption discloses unreliable time as its own term, and unreliable segments surface as a
seventh individually-disclosed per-activity quality signal, badged with its measured value.

**Requirements:** SEG-06, PACE-08, PACE-09, COV-03 — plus Phase 33's own half of SEG-05b (see
D-15).

**Not in this phase:** pause classification by implied speed and forgotten stops (Phase 34),
stream-derived running pace and the disputed-pace threshold (Phase 35), best-effort partitioning,
demotion and the ceiling diff (Phase 36), review-queue priority (Phase 37). No stream file is
rewritten, no distance, total or split figure is altered — clipping withholds a claim, it never
corrects one.

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### The coverage ledger and the fourth GapKind

- **D-01:** **The whole merged segment becomes `unreliable` time.** A segment's full
  `[startSec, endSec]` — burst seconds *and* the seconds of any gap it merged — becomes one
  `unreliable` interval, sitting at the TOP of `classifyGaps`' strict-priority tree (above
  `recording-gap`, above `pause`). Gap-adjacent time therefore moves OUT of `recordingGapSec` /
  `pauseSec`. Rationale: SEG-02 already makes the merged segment the unit of truth that conserves
  real distance and real elapsed time, so "how long was this segment" must have exactly one
  answer. Measured cost, to be disclosed rather than discovered: ~11,574 s across 63 gap-adjacent
  activities moves between shipped caption terms. Rejected: burst-seconds-only (purely additive,
  no shipped figure moves, but it itemises one segment across two categories and leaves the
  caption calling contaminated gap time a plain recording gap).
- **D-02:** **Widen `GapKind`, do not add a parallel array.** `GapKind` gains `'unreliable'`,
  `PaceCoverage` gains `unreliableSec`, and unreliable intervals land in the SAME `gapIntervals`
  array. Window clipping (`derivePaceSeriesGapAware`), histogram exclusion
  (`paceHistogramSamples`) and split annotation then inherit the behaviour with zero call-site
  changes, which is what the ROADMAP goal's "zero code change outside `pace-derivation.ts`" means
  (D-12). The identity becomes `coveredSec + recordingGapSec + pauseSec + unreliableSec ===
  spanSec`. Rejected: a separate `unreliableIntervals` array — nothing would clip until every
  consumer was updated, which is the code change outside `pace-derivation.ts` the goal rules out.
- **D-03:** **The archive-wide identity proof is a regenerable artifact, not a suite scan.** The
  archive-wide scan lives in a committed, idempotent artifact generated by extending the
  detector-free `scripts/compute-unreliable-segment-recount.mjs` across the whole archive; it also
  states how much time moved out of `recording-gap` / `pause` per mechanism. `npm test` keeps a
  fast named-fixture identity guard plus the existing single-activity crosscheck. **Note for the
  planner:** `coveredSec + … === spanSec` is near-tautological and proves little on its own — the
  load-bearing assertion is that `unreliableSec` matches an INDEPENDENTLY recounted segment-seconds
  total (the D-15/gap-first algorithm), not that `classifyGaps` agrees with itself. Rejected:
  running the full 1,874-stream scan every `npm test` (suite is already serialised with
  `fileParallelism: false`), and doing both (two places the number is computed).

### `gapProfileSignal` (COV-03's explicit decision)

- **D-04:** **Unreliable time enters `gapFraction`'s numerator.**
  `gapFraction = (recordingGapSec + pauseSec + unreliableSec) / spanSec`. Measured reasoning: under
  D-01 the 63 gap-adjacent activities keep EXACTLY the fraction they have today, so no shipped
  badge is silently lost; hidden-pause adds ~25 s and isolated-jump ~4.8 s per activity, both
  fractions of a percent. Excluding it could only SHRINK the fraction for those 63 activities,
  dropping severe/minor badges purely because of a re-label. The signal keeps its meaning: how much
  of this span is not trustworthy covered time.
- **D-05:** **`GAP_PROFILE_SEVERE_FRACTION` (0.20) and `GAP_PROFILE_MINOR_FRACTION` (0.05) stay
  frozen.** The before/after per-tier migration is measured archive-wide and reported as a finding
  in the same artifact — **including if it is zero**. Phase 27's D-02 anti-quota rule already
  forbids moving a constant to change a count; it is restated here because a reader meeting a
  changed numerator will be tempted to "recalibrate". Rejected: re-deriving both cuts against the
  new four-way distribution.

### SEG-06 — the seventh quality signal

- **D-06:** **Tiered, and structurally excluded from the severe composite.** The signal gets
  `none` / `minor` / `severe`, badges a list row only at `severe`, and `hasAnySevereSignal`'s
  `Pick<>` is **NEVER** widened to include it — the exact Phase 30 D-06 elevation precedent,
  including its never-widen guard test (demonstrate by temporarily widening, watch the guard fail,
  revert). This keeps v2.2's composite figure of record (299 of 1,890, 15.8%) comparable across
  milestones. Measured reason a binary presence signal was rejected: 699 of 1,874 activities
  (37.3%) carry at least one segment, so presence-as-severe would badge over a third of the archive
  and roughly triple the composite. Rejected: joining the composite (defensible on the grounds that
  unreliable segments are a pace-trust signal in a way elevation is not, but it moves the composite
  and the list filter it drives); an untiered fact like `deviceEra` (nothing would surface on a list
  row even for a 199 m hidden pause).
- **D-07:** **The tier is measured on EXCESS metres as a fraction of the activity's own
  distance.** Excess = distance carried beyond what `UNRELIABLE_SPEED_LIMIT_MPS` allows in the
  segments' own elapsed time — the detector's existing D-09 concept, not a new one. One new
  constant, derived in the same artifact with its own non-circularity argument and D-13
  insensitivity check (re-derive with detected segments removed, and with planted synthetics
  added; a constant that moves beyond a stated tolerance is a failed calibration, watched failing
  first on a deliberately circular strawman). The archive-wide severe rate this produces is
  reported as a finding, never tuned toward a target. Rejected: unreliable-seconds ÷ span (mirrors
  `gapProfile`'s shape, but scores 3475734189's 199 m hidden pause at ~0.2% of span —
  structurally unable to call the archive's worst distance lie severe); a two-part either-clears
  rule (catches both shapes, costs two constants to derive and keep insensitive).
- **D-08:** **Row scalars for the badge, shard segments for the evidence.** The dashboard index row
  gains, additively with `DASHBOARD_INDEX_SCHEMA_VERSION` unchanged at `1`: tier, segment count,
  total metres, total excess metres, total seconds, and which mechanisms fired — enough for the
  badge's measured value with no shard fetch. The lazy quality shard gains the per-segment list
  **without** `parts` (`startSec`/`endSec`, metres, excess metres, seconds, mechanism), capped at
  100 entries like `impossibleSamples`. Follows D-17's "a profile, not full per-sample arrays"
  discipline; measured scale is ~2 segments per flagged activity (1,344 over 699). Rejected: full
  `UnreliableSegment` objects including `parts` (compute-time shape leaking into the wire format);
  row scalars only (the evidence line could only restate the badge and could never name WHERE in
  the run the segments sit).

### Rendered surfaces and copy

- **D-09:** **One split marker; unreliable takes priority when present.** `SplitGapAnnotation`
  gains `unreliableSec`. The marker stays single — preserving the UI-SPEC's one-category-per-split
  copy contract — but `unreliable` wins whenever present rather than competing on seconds;
  `recording gap` and `pause` keep today's magnitude rule (and today's tie-favours-recording-gap
  rule) between themselves. Reason: a split holding a 200 s pause and a 2.7 s isolated jump would,
  under pure magnitude, name only the pause and never mention the jump that corrupted that split's
  pace. Rejected: three-way magnitude dominance; a second distinct marker per split (most honest,
  but breaks the copy contract and adds a glyph to every affected splits row).
- **D-10:** **The caption gains a fourth always-on term.** `coverageCaptionText` becomes
  `{c}% of elapsed time covered · {r}% recording gaps · {p}% paused · {u}% unreliable`, rendered
  even at 0% so a clean run states its own health — COV-02's always-on rule, where an absent term
  would stop meaning "zero" and start meaning "unknown". Independent per-term rounding stays: the
  four integers still need not sum to 100, and must never be forced to, since that would falsify an
  individual value against COV-01's exact-second sum. Rejected: `unreliable segments` (longer, on a
  heading line already carrying three terms); rendering the term only when non-zero.
- **D-11:** **The existing gap-profile copy widens to name all three components.** Because D-04
  puts unreliable time inside the number, today's strings misdescribe it. Badge `visibleText`
  becomes `{n}% of recorded time in gaps, pauses or unreliable segments`; the detail row label
  becomes `Coverage gaps`; the healthy text becomes `No gaps, pauses or unreliable segments`. This
  honours the badge's own docblock rule — the text names exactly what the number counts. Rejected:
  a category-neutral phrase (`Coverage` / "not trustworthy") which drops the itemisation that
  docblock exists to protect; leaving the copy alone, which is the precise defect it warns against.

### Scope, evidence and process

- **D-12:** **The ROADMAP Phase 33 goal gets a dated in-place amendment.** The phrase "with zero
  code change outside `pace-derivation.ts`" is true of the CLIPPING mechanism (PACE-08) and false
  of the phase: PACE-09's marker, COV-03's caption and SEG-06's signal necessarily touch
  `detail-sections.ts`, `pace-quality.ts`, the index row and the shard. Amend the goal in place,
  narrowing the phrase to the clipping mechanism, with dated provenance — the Phase 32 D-03 pattern
  that amended ROADMAP criterion 4 and REQUIREMENTS SEG-04. Reason for amending the file rather than
  only noting it here: the verifier reads `ROADMAP.md`, and would otherwise score every
  `detail-sections.ts` edit as a goal violation. ROADMAP/STATE mutations go through
  `gsd-sdk query` handlers where one exists.
- **D-13:** **Checkpoint rows assert an independently-derived extent, not an absence.** For each of
  3475724402, 3475734189 and 3475740390, compute the expected post-clip FASTEST pace from raw
  `t`/`d` OUTSIDE the changed code, and have the row read back the rendered fastest hovered tooltip
  and compare against that value — not merely "the 0:34/km sample is gone". Add one control
  activity with no detected segments whose full range must still render, and give every row a
  stated CAN FAIL / CAN PASS reachability proof before it is run. Reason: an absence-only row passes
  equally well against a chart that failed to render at all, and this project has twice shipped a
  green gate over a broken build on exactly that shape. Rejected: asserting only the named samples'
  absence.
- **D-14:** **The detector runs in the browser path too — one code path.** `classifyGaps` calls
  `detectUnreliableSegments` wherever it runs, browser and CI alike, so the caption, chart,
  histogram and splits agree by construction (the same structural argument that makes
  `derivePaceWithCoverage` return pace and coverage together). Decisive practical reason: the
  quality shard is lazy-fetched AFTER first paint, so a shard-fed chart would have to render before
  its segments arrived. Cost is one O(n) pass per detail view and the detector module in the
  bundle. Rejected: passing CI-precomputed segments into `classifyGaps` (a second source of truth
  needing its own agreement proof, on an asynchronous arrival).
- **D-15:** **SEG-05b's premature tick is corrected in this phase.** `REQUIREMENTS.md:24` carries
  SEG-05b as `[x]` with a traceability row reading **Complete**, while the row's own text says it
  "stays Pending until every consuming phase (33, 34/35, 36) has shown its consumer reading the
  detector" — and Phase 32 had no consumer at all, so nothing could have satisfied it. Untick the
  row and set its traceability cell back to Pending with a dated provenance note naming the
  contradiction. Phase 33 then records its own half: `pace-derivation.ts` reading the detector,
  proven **load-bearing** by watching the clipping disappear when the call is stubbed — not by the
  import merely existing. Re-tick only after Phases 35 and 36 record theirs.
- **D-16:** **D-07's severe cut is derived from a non-self-referential reference population
  (developer decision, 2026-09-21, resolving plan 33-08's HALT).** Plan 33-08 ended in its own
  documented HALT branch (Task 3 step 5): every candidate outer percentile (0.90 / 0.95 / 0.97 /
  0.99) of the 699 flagged activities' excess-fraction population failed 32-CONTEXT.md D-13
  insensitivity against the fixed 1% tolerance (removedDelta 50–77%, plantedDelta 55–4333%).
  Measured root cause, structural rather than a near miss: the percentiled population IS the
  flagged set, so "re-derive with detected segments removed" collapses the population itself, and
  the real q=0.99 candidate is literally the same computation as the deliberately circular strawman.
  The distribution is heavily right-skewed with no plateau (31 of 699 exactly 0, median 0.00127, max
  0.109), so no order statistic of it is stable. **Chosen — option 1, a different statistic SHAPE:**
  the cut is derived from a reference population that is NOT the flagged set (ordinary folded
  covered steps and per-activity distances over all computable activities — the Phase 32 lesson that
  rescued `UNRELIABLE_EXCESS_FLOOR_M`), with D-07's measured quantity (excess metres ÷ the
  activity's own distance), D-13's two-direction check and the 1% tolerance all kept intact. The
  candidate shapes are pre-declared in plan 33-14's text as a short fixed ordered list, each with a
  written non-circularity argument BEFORE any measurement; D-13 arbitrates by first-clearing order;
  the archive-wide severe rate is a reported finding and may never select, tune or re-order a shape
  (Phase 27 D-02 anti-quota). If no pre-declared shape clears, plan 33-14 HALTS again on a committed
  NO SHAPE CLEARS artifact — nothing is widened or loosened unilaterally. 33-08's failed percentile
  table stays in `33-CALIBRATION.md` as a recorded negative finding. Rejected: **lower percentiles
  of the same flagged population** (still a rank of the judged population — the same circular shape
  at a different order, and it reclassifies more activities as severe by construction); **a wider
  tolerance** (the nearest miss is 50.35%, over 50× the tolerance — no justification survives that
  margin, and the tolerance was fixed before the check precisely so it could not be moved after);
  **a declared product threshold** (abandons measured-not-chosen for the one constant SEG-06's tier
  rests on, with no insensitivity evidence at all).

## Existing Code Insights

### Reusable Assets
- `classifyGaps` already emits merged, real-time `GapInterval` runs with `startSec`/`endSec` — the
  detector's `UnreliableSegment` carries the same field names, so the ledger extension is a
  type-widening plus a third branch in one loop, not a rewrite.
- `derivePaceSeriesGapAware` clips on `gapIntervals` generically (inside-gap → `null`, window
  clamped at boundaries). Under D-02, PACE-08's clipping is inherited rather than implemented.
- `paceHistogramSamples` already excludes any segment whose OWN START falls inside a gap interval —
  the subtle case a naive `paceSeries[i] === null` check misses. Inherited too.
- `appendAccessibleBadge` / `qualityBadgeSpecs` / `elevationBadgeContent` — the fourth badge source
  (elevation) is the exact template for a fifth.
- `quantile` (R-7, `pace-derivation.ts:100`) — the percentile definition to reuse for D-07. Do not
  add a third.

### Established Patterns
- Only `severe`-tier signals badge a list row; `none` and `not-computable` stay silent there and
  are disclosed in the detail view's always-on Quality Signals section instead.
- A tiered signal may be deliberately excluded from `hasAnySevereSignal` — elevation does exactly
  this, with a guard test that fails if the `Pick<>` is widened.
- Constants carry a docblock naming the measurement they came from and the rule forbidding tuning
  toward a count.
- Every calibration generator has a `.test.mjs`, is run twice to prove idempotence, and its prose
  is generated — hand-editing the artifact without fixing the generator silently reverts.
- Guards are demonstrated red on a planted violation before they may pass.

### Integration Points — and three silent-inheritance traps
- **`splitGapAnnotations`' else-branch is two-way**: `if (gap.kind === 'recording-gap') … else
  pauseSec += …`. Under D-02, an unreliable interval would silently be counted as pause. Must be
  widened explicitly (D-09), and a test must pin that it is.
- **`gapProfileRow`'s evidence line** reads `shard.gapIntervals` and reports "N gap intervals;
  longest …". Under D-02 that list now contains unreliable intervals, so the count silently
  changes. Decide and pin whether it filters by kind.
- **`qualityBadgeSpecs`' gap-profile badge text** ("% of recorded time in gaps or pauses") states
  what its own number counts; D-04 changes the number, so D-11's copy widening is not cosmetic —
  it is a correctness fix.
- `npm test` (vitest, `fileParallelism: false`) is the nightly deploy gate: nothing added there may
  read gitignored or owner-editable live files (Phase 31 TD-01).
- `data/streams/` immutability is gated by `compute-stream-checksums`; this phase writes nothing
  there.
