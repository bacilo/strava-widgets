## Diagnostic Finding (reported, not acted on): why W1 landed at 6.95 m/s

Not a fix, not a re-parameterisation — a direct measurement of the population W1 actually sums, run to help the developer's decision, discarded from selection per D-08/anti-quota.

The valley rule picks the qualifying bin with the GLOBAL LOWEST count anywhere in `[STATIONARY_MAX_SPEED_MPS, UNRELIABLE_SPEED_LIMIT_MPS)` = `[0.5, 7.35)`, bin width 0.1 m/s. Dumping W1's own histogram (folded-step seconds per bin, archive-wide) shows:

- A single unimodal hump, peaking at bin `[3.00, 3.10)` with 852,973 seconds, rising smoothly from `[0.50,0.60)` (6,321 s) with **no dip anywhere near the developer-confirmed walking-break speeds** (1.26–1.80 m/s, bins 11–13, which show a smooth, monotonically rising 40,612 → 63,901 s — walking and running form one continuous hump here, not two separated clusters).
- The hump decays into a long, sparse tail from ~4.0 m/s onward, with small periodic count spikes roughly every 0.5 m/s (bin `[5.00,5.10)` = 11,484 s vs neighbours 4,008/3,604 s; `[5.50,5.60)` = 7,627 s vs neighbours 2,086/1,555 s; `[6.00,6.10)` = 3,389 s vs neighbours 984/598 s; `[6.50,6.60)` = 1,436 s vs neighbours 384/413 s) — a pattern consistent with decimated-stream speed quantisation, not measured here beyond noting it.
- The GLOBAL minimum qualifying bin sits at `[6.90, 7.00)` with only 309 seconds archive-wide (the next-lowest qualifying bins are `[6.80,6.90)` at 312 s and `[6.60,6.70)`-family bins in the 380–600 s range) — i.e. the rule found the single sparsest bin in the whole range, deep in the tail approaching the 7.35 m/s unreliable-speed ceiling, not a genuine walk/run transition.
- This is corroborated directly by the artifact's own rendered per-interval table for 3475724402 (criterion 1's named activity): at the candidate 6.95 m/s boundary, **all 18 of its merged-segment/recording-gap intervals (2.307–4.065 m/s) classify `walked`, none `ran-through`** — contradicting D-02's own stated expectation ("3475724402's merged segments sit at 2.3–4.1 m/s — continued running through signal loss... expected to split between walked and ran-through per D-06's valley, never pinned to one class").

## Blocking Developer Decision

**`WALK_RUN_BOUNDARY_MPS` (Family W, D-06) cannot ship from this artifact as written — the first time any family has cleared its own D-13 arbitration in this phase, but the shipped value fails a post-selection gate.** Per this plan's own halt protocol (mirroring the 33-08/32-05/34-06-HALT-1/34-06-HALT-2 precedent), no shape, order, tolerance, margin, declared value or gate bound was widened, loosened, reordered, retuned or hand-picked after seeing this delta.

**What the numbers show.** Both pre-declared W shapes (W1 time-weighted, W2 count-weighted) clear D-13 insensitivity cleanly (0.0000% on both axes) and agree closely (6.95 vs 6.85 m/s) — the D-13 harness itself is not the problem. The problem is downstream: the valley-finding rule (pre-declared, unchanged since 34-04) picks the single sparsest bin anywhere in `[STATIONARY_MAX_SPEED_MPS, UNRELIABLE_SPEED_LIMIT_MPS)`, and on this archive's own covered-time speed distribution that sparsest bin sits in a long, thin, structurally artifact-laden tail near the unreliable-speed ceiling (6.90–7.00 m/s, 309 seconds out of 2.6 million total) rather than at any genuine walk/run transition. The distribution itself shows no dip near the developer-confirmed walking-break speeds (1.26–1.80 m/s) — walking and running form one continuous, unimodal hump there. Gate D-12(ii) — which independently checks that a confirmed walking-break control's OWN rest-of-activity running pace clears the shipped boundary — catches exactly this: 2.8257 m/s (an easy-running pace, consistent with the developer's own ~3.4 m/s reference and 3475724402's 2.31–4.06 m/s segments) does not clear 6.95 m/s.

**Three measured, named options — none chosen here:**

1. **A different pre-declared shape or population for Family W.** The current shapes (W1/W2) both pool ALL covered-time folded steps archive-wide and hand the valley-picking rule the WHOLE `[0.5, 7.35)` range unconstrained, so a shape whose population is restricted to a narrower, more plausible sub-range (e.g., excluding the sparse tail beyond some structurally-argued point), or whose statistic does not pick the single global-minimum bin (e.g., the first qualifying bin working outward from the walking-speed side, or a bimodality/clustering statistic instead of a prominence-dip rule), might land closer to where the developer-confirmed walking breaks and 3475724402's own segments actually sit. This needs its own non-circularity argument — not this plan's to write.
2. **Investigate the tail's periodic count spikes first**, as a finding rather than a re-fixture: bins centred near 5.05, 5.55, 6.05, 6.55 and 7.05 m/s each show a local 2–4x count spike relative to their immediate neighbours, which is what feeds the GLOBAL-minimum rule's choice of exactly where in the tail it lands. Understanding whether this is a genuine archive property (e.g., a decimation/quantisation artifact on certain streams) before any shape is redeclared could materially change which bin ends up sparsest.
3. **A stated wider D-12(ii) bound, a different valley-picking rule (first-qualifying instead of global-minimum), or a narrower `hi` bound** is named per this plan's own requirement to list every measured option, but is judged the least promising here on the measured numbers: the shipped 6.95 m/s is not a near-miss of a plausible walk/run boundary — it is roughly 2–2.5x the developer's own reference running pace (~3.4 m/s) and sits in a 309-second artifact tail, not a genuine transition. Loosening a bound to accommodate it would not correct the underlying valley-picking issue named in options 1–2.

**Options explicitly not available**, per this plan's own halt protocol: widening the fixed 1% `INSENSITIVITY_TOLERANCE`, changing the `PLANT_FRACTION` formula, changing the `±0.02` D-10 margin, changing the declared `0.5` (D-20), falling back to a later/rejected shape, or picking a value by looking at the measured deltas.
