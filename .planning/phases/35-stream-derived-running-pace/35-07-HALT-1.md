## Which axis failed to bite, and why (measured, not narrated)

Axis A genuinely bites for S0 (it fails, as designed) — but Axis A is never counted by `shapeClears`, by the pre-declaration's own explicit rule (`shapeClears` reads only `axisB`/`axisC`). **Axis B and Axis C — the only two axes that count — both failed to bite.**

The measured reason: `strawmanCutS0`'s value is `delta(3475724402) − smallestPositiveGap(sortedDeltas(E))`. Both perturbations Axis B/C apply act on the TAIL of E's delta distribution (Axis B plants 13 copies of E's own maximum, 243.2491; Axis C removes E's 13 largest members). Neither perturbation touches where the population's own smallest positive gap between adjacent sorted deltas actually sits — which, on a dense 1291-item population, is overwhelmingly likely to be somewhere in the crowded middle of the distribution (median-adjacent region), not the sparse tail. Adding or removing 13 tail items changes neither the sorted-middle structure nor, therefore, the smallest gap — hence `plantedCut === trimmedCut === baseCut` exactly, to four decimal places, i.e. genuinely `0.0000%` movement on both axes, not a rounding coincidence.

This is a structural property of the `smallest-positive-gap` statistic itself, not an archive-specific fluke found by trial and error: this generator's own 35-06 test suite already demonstrated (synthetically) that S0 CAN be made to clear when the target's own delta sits in an isolated pocket far from both the plant location (population max) and the trim location (population top 1%) — see 35-06-SUMMARY.md's "patterns-established" note. The real archive independently reproduces that exact shape: E is dense enough, and S0's own statistic is defined in a way that is inherently insensitive to tail perturbation regardless of what the tail contains.
