STANDSTILL DEFINITIONS (new; base data only, never perturbed)
- P_a, per computable activity = `PAUSE_GAP_P90_MULTIPLIER x quantile(sorted advanceIntervals(t, d),
  0.90)`. This is exactly the threshold `classifyPairKinds` applies. A pause interval is one of the
  activity's zero-advance pause intervals (`view.pauseIntervals`). The pause rule marks a
  zero-advance run as pause iff its duration is strictly greater than P_a.
- Bridge: a `CoveredRun` r of the same activity such that `t[r.startIndex]` equals the `endSec` of
  a pause interval, `t[r.endIndex]` equals the `startSec` of a pause interval, and
  `t[r.endIndex] - t[r.startIndex] < P_a`. Bridge metres = `d[r.endIndex] - d[r.startIndex]`.
  Invariant, asserted in `buildBaseViews` (throw on violation): every bridge has metres > 0.
  Two distinct maximal zero-advance runs must be separated by at least one advance, otherwise they
  would be one run. A zero-metre bridge therefore means the views are wrong.
- Standstill episode: a maximal alternating chain `p1, b1, p2, ..., bk-1, pk` of pause intervals
  and bridges, each adjacent to the next. Anything else between two pauses breaks the chain: a
  recording gap, a merged or unreliable segment, or a covered run of P_a or longer. Its span is
  `[p1.startSec, pk.endSec]`, seconds = span, metres = sum of bridge metres (pauses contribute 0).
  Rate = metres / seconds. The episode is CHAINED iff k >= 2.
- Only chained episodes form the G4/G5 population. A chained episode's rate is strictly > 0 by
  the invariant, so neither shape can produce a zero base from real items.
- Structural bound, used in the D-12 argument: each pause lasts more than P_a and each bridge
  less than P_a. So bridges occupy strictly less than half of any episode's seconds, and an
  episode's rate is strictly less than half the fastest speed of its bridges.

G FAMILY (re-declared). Shipped as `STATIONARY_MAX_SPEED_MPS`, rounding unchanged. ORDER criteria,
in priority:
(1) stillness is decided by a speed-free rule the classifier's bands never read (the duration-only
    zero-advance pause rule);
(2) the statistic's unit matches what the constant judges in PAUSE-01: one interval's aggregate
    metres / seconds;
(3) no activity dominates;
(4) fewest inherited caveats.
G4 and G5 tie on (1). G4 wins on (2), since one item is one standstill interval, while G5's item
is an activity-level ratio. G5 wins on (3). Priority order gives G4 first, G5 second.

G4: "standstill episodes, pooled".
  Population: every chained standstill episode, pooled archive-wide. Statistic: R-7 p90 of their
  rates.
  Meaning: 90% of the archive's established standstills in which the odometer drifted at all
  accrued drift at or below this rate. Above it the odometer is advancing faster than all but the
  top tenth of real standstills, so something is moving.
  Non-circularity: membership comes only from the pause rule (Phase 26, duration-only, no speed
  term) and covered-run adjacency. Neither reads a band, a class or a speed. No J interval can lie
  inside an episode, because a bridge is a covered run and any gap-bearing interval between two
  pauses breaks the chain. The band judges J (never pause intervals, D-01) and, through STOP-01,
  individual covered pairs. The statistic is an aggregate over whole standstills including their
  zero-advance time, never a percentile of judged items. The unchanged REMOVED view measures what
  leakage remains.
  Why it sits below walking, argued without any measured Phase 34 number:
  (a) A pause needs a zero-advance run longer than P_a, five times the activity's own p90 advance
      interval. A person walking advances the odometer at the device's normal cadence, so walking
      cannot produce the pauses an episode is made of. That is the premise of the pause rule
      itself. A sustained walking window contains no pause and is structurally never an episode.
  (b) By the structural bound, an episode's rate is under half its fastest bridge speed. For an
      episode to reach walking pace, the athlete must move at more than twice walking pace
      between stops, each time for less than P_a. The p90 reaches walking pace only if more than a
      tenth of chained episodes are such stop-sprint-stop patterns. Were that so, the unchanged
      D-12 gate (i) would HALT the run; nothing wrong would ship.
  Caveats:
  (c1) It sees light drift only. A standstill forms pauses only where drift ticks are further
       apart than P_a. Heavier drift forms no pause, so it is invisible here and the value may be
       low. That is conservative for walking breaks, and forgotten stops with dense drift may be
       under-detected.
  (c2) Aggregate versus pair. By D-07 the same constant also bounds individual covered-pair speeds
       in creep detection. A single drift tick's pair speed exceeds an episode's average, so ticks
       can split creep runs. This is the same under-detection direction as (c1), disclosed and not
       corrected.
  (c3) Conditional on drift. A single-pause standstill accrues 0 by construction and is excluded.
       It constrains no ceiling, being stationary under any cut >= 0. Including it would risk a
       zero base, which the unchanged harness scores FAIL, turning "drift is rare" into a spurious
       HALT.
  (c4) Overlap with G3, stated as D-19 requires. Bridges lie inside G3's old pause-boundary
       windows whenever they are shorter than the neighbourhood width, so the two populations
       share covered pairs. G4 is NOT "G3 with the neighbourhood removed":
       - it pre-excludes nothing near J, so its REMOVED row is live;
       - its statistic is a whole-standstill aggregate including zero-advance time, not a pair
         median;
       - a single stop's run-in and run-out pairs (G3's deceleration/acceleration mixture) never
         enter, because only covered runs sandwiched between two pauses qualify.
  (c5) Small population: at most half the archive's pause intervals. Pauses often adjoin
       recording gaps (auto-pause after a stop), so the REMOVED view may drop many episodes. That
       is a real sensitivity for the harness to measure, not a defect.

G5: "standstill episodes, per activity".
  Population: activities with at least one chained episode. That is a structural qualification,
  not a new constant. Statistic: per activity, total chained-episode metres / total chained-episode
  seconds (that activity's own drift rate over its drifting standstills). Cut = R-7 p90 across
  activities.
  Meaning: the same ceiling, with each activity voting once, so an activity with many stops cannot
  dominate G4's pooled statistic.
  Non-circularity: as G4. Why it sits below walking: as G4 (a) and (b). A ratio of episode sums
  inherits the half-bridge-speed bound, because each episode satisfies it.
  Caveats: as G4 (c1)-(c5). It ranks below G4 on criterion (2), being a two-level statistic. Each
  planted item lifts exactly one host activity's ratio, which D-13's planted row measures.
  Reachability is argued below. Plants go only to member activities, so planting never creates a
  member.

PLANTED VIEW (replaces 34-04's bullet; ALL families, uniform, D-19):
- Source shapes: unchanged. `{ metres, seconds }` of every J interval on 3475724402.
- Separately for every shape and every strawman: N = the shape's base item count (`items`), in
  the unit plants enter, before any per-activity qualification. Plant count =
  `ceil(PLANT_FRACTION x N)`, with `PLANT_FRACTION = INSENSITIVITY_TOLERANCE` (assigned, never a
  literal). N = 0 gives 0 plants, and the base is null, so FAIL anyway. N >= 1 gives at least one
  plant, so the row is never vacuous.
- Why proportional (counting only): once plants outnumber a population's real items, every
  quantile's rank lands inside the plants. The old |J| rule therefore made any population much
  smaller than |J| unpassable whatever its statistic, and an unpassable check certifies nothing.
  The same fixed count was a negligible fraction of a large population. A proportional count
  gives every shape the same strength of test.
- Why this fraction: D-13 asks whether a leak of judged-like items destabilises the statistic,
  with "stable" already fixed as moving by at most 1%. Setting the leak equal to the tolerance
  asks "does a 1% leak move it by more than 1%?", i.e. the statistic may not amplify
  contamination. It adds no free number, and it was not chosen by trying values.
- Why uniform: D-13 is "one harness for every shape and strawman". M and W have never been
  measured, so no data could favour treating them differently. Recorded: M's and W's planted rule
  changed with this amendment. Whether they now receive more or fewer plants than under the old
  rule is unknown and was not looked at.
- Placement: plants cycle the source shapes in order. Targets go round-robin over the activities,
  sorted by id, that contribute at least one base item to THAT shape's population. A leak enters
  a population where it lives.
- Membership: a plant is ONE item of the population's unit, flagged `planted: true`, with the
  source metres and seconds and speed = metres / seconds. It enters that shape's population
  UNCONDITIONALLY: no positional, cadence or adjacency filter may exclude it. This generalises
  34-04's "treated as cadence-silent (worst case)". Units:
  - chained episode: G4, G5
  - J interval: strawman G, strawman W
  - advance interval of length `seconds`: M1, M2
  - creep-candidate duration of `seconds`: strawman M
  - folded step: W1, W2
  After entry, the statistic treats a plant like any real item (e.g. W's histogram bins it only if
  its speed is in `[lo, hi)`). Per-activity statistics and qualification are re-derived on the
  perturbed view, as before.
- Non-vacuity (added): each row renders its plant count, and a test asserts
  `planted.items === base.items + plantCount` for every shape and strawman.

REMOVED VIEW for G4/G5: 34-04's unchanged rule ("drop every reference-population item whose span
intersects the neighbourhood of any J interval of the same activity"), with the item being the
whole chained episode and its span `[p1.startSec, pk.endSec]`. Episodes are built once from base
data and never re-chained after removal.

is non-empty, since chained rates are > 0. If there is no chained episode at all, the base is null,
